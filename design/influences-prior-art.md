# Commons Protocol — Influences and Prior Art
### v0.1 / internal / not for publication

---

## Purpose of this document

This document acknowledges the intellectual lineage of the Commons Protocol. It records the thinkers, projects, and prior work that have directly shaped the protocol's design philosophy, biological models, governance architecture, and technical approach.

Acknowledgement here is not endorsement of every position held by every cited source, nor does it imply that any cited individual or organisation has endorsed or is affiliated with this project. It is an honest record of intellectual debt — and an invitation to those whose work appears here to engage with the project if they choose.

---

## Foundational intellectual influences

### Paul Hawken — *Blessed Unrest* (2007)

Hawken's central claim — that environmental protection, human rights, and social justice constitute a single distributed global movement, the largest in human history, operating without central command or shared manifesto — is load-bearing for the Commons Protocol's "One Movement" framing. The immune system metaphor, which Hawken develops as a biological analogue for this distributed response to systemic threat, directly informs the protocol's self-understanding as infrastructure for an already-existing immune response rather than the initiator of a new one.

Hawken's subsequent work with **Project Drawdown** — the most rigorous existing framework for quantifying and prioritising climate solutions — is identified in the interconnection map design document as a priority institutional relationship and a methodological reference for the map's climate connection layer.

### Dr Elisabet Sahtouris — *EarthDance: Living Systems in Evolution* (iUniverse, 2000)

Sahtouris's work on living systems as models for human economic and social design provides the ATP framework that runs through the founding document and the token and funding layer design. Her argument that the equitable, non-accumulative, flow-oriented distribution of energy in biological systems is not merely a metaphor but a design principle applicable to human institutions is the conceptual foundation for the protocol's economic architecture — both in the unmonetised signal layer and in the CEU token model.

### R. Buckminster Fuller

The epigraph to the founding document — *"You never change things by fighting the existing reality. To change something, build a new model that makes the existing model obsolete"* — establishes the protocol's fundamental strategic orientation. The Commons Protocol is not a protest movement, a petition platform, or an advocacy campaign. It is an attempt to build infrastructure that makes the existing model of concentrated, suppressible, easily manipulated public opinion legible in a new way. Fuller's principle is the north star.

### Robin Dunbar — *Grooming, Gossip, and the Evolution of Language* (Harvard University Press, 1996)

Dunbar's research on the cognitive limits of stable social groups — approximately 150 members, a figure that holds across hunter-gatherer societies, military units, and corporate organisations — provides the empirical basis for the local tier's community size in the filter pyramid. The ~150 member limit is not arbitrary. It reflects the scale at which genuine social knowledge of fellow participants is cognitively possible, which is the scale at which authentic community-level consensus is meaningful rather than performed.

### Shoshana Zuboff — *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (2019)

Zuboff's documentation of the structural incentives driving the attention economy — attention as commodity, engagement as profit, outrage as fuel — provides the evidential basis for the founding document's characterisation of the existing information infrastructure as structurally amplified noise rather than genuine global conversation. Her analysis of surveillance capitalism as a distinct economic logic, not merely a set of corporate bad practices, informs the protocol's insistence that structural separation from commercial incentives is the only reliable protection for signal integrity.

### Tim Wu — *The Attention Merchants* (2016)

Wu's historical account of the commodification of human attention — from nineteenth century newspapers through broadcast media to social platforms — contextualises the current information environment as the latest iteration of a long pattern rather than a novel crisis. This historical framing informs the protocol's claim that a genuinely different model is needed, not merely better regulation of the existing one.

### Naomi Oreskes and Erik Conway — *Merchants of Doubt* (2010)

Oreskes and Conway's documentation of coordinated campaigns to manufacture scientific uncertainty — in tobacco research, climate science, and elsewhere — is foundational to the evidence record design document's epistemic bias section. Their work establishes the pattern: capture the credentialing system, then use it to exclude challenges. This pattern is what the evidence record's hybrid model is designed to partially address, and what the document honestly acknowledges it cannot fully resolve in the first iteration.

### Ben Goldacre — *Bad Pharma* (2012)

Goldacre's systematic documentation of commercial influence on pharmaceutical research — selective publication of favourable results, suppression of unfavourable ones, ghost-writing of academic papers — provides specific evidential grounding for the evidence record design document's acknowledgement that peer review is not a sufficient credibility standard in domains where commercial interests are strong. Cited alongside Oreskes and Conway as a named case of the epistemic bias problem.

## Prior art in protocol and platform design

### The Fediverse / ActivityPub Protocol

The federated social web protocol underlying Mastodon and related platforms provides the closest existing technical infrastructure to what the Commons Protocol requires: no single server, no single owner, interoperable across instances, resistant to suppression by any single actor. The Commons Protocol's "no central authority" principle finds its most relevant technical expression in the ActivityPub model. Identified as a candidate infrastructure layer in the founding document and governance sketch.

### Nostr

A more radically decentralised alternative to ActivityPub — a censorship-resistant communication protocol in which identity is a cryptographic keypair owned by the participant rather than an account held by a platform. Nostr's identity model is directly relevant to the verification sketch's device-based zero-knowledge proof pathway and the self-sovereign identity principle. Identified as a candidate infrastructure and identity layer.

### Consul Project (consulproject.org)

An open-source participatory democracy platform developed by the Madrid city government, now deployed in dozens of cities globally. Consul's mechanisms for participatory budgeting, citizen proposals, and deliberative processes are the most relevant existing prior art for the Commons Protocol's question elevation process and local tier governance. The filter pyramid's design draws on Consul's experience of managing question volume and quality in large urban participatory processes.

### BrightID

A social graph-based proof-of-personhood system that allows participants to establish unique human identity through mutual verification in group video calls, without centralised identity data. BrightID is identified in the verification sketch as one of the candidate proof-of-personhood pathways for Tier 2 and above verification. Its design directly addresses the uniqueness-without-disclosure constraint that the verification sketch names as the central design challenge.

### Gitcoin

A platform for funding open-source development through community grants and quadratic funding mechanisms. Identified in the token and funding layer document as the closest existing model for the CEU token — with the critical distinction that the Commons Protocol's ATP non-accumulation constraint prohibits the secondary market trading that has affected Gitcoin's GTC token. Gitcoin's experience with contributor compensation and infrastructure funding governance is relevant prior art for the token layer design.

### Metagovernance Project (metagov.org)

An academic and practitioner research collective working on digital governance, online community design, and the governance of platforms and protocols. The Metagovernance Project's research directly addresses the governance design challenges named as open problems in the governance sketch and tiered access specification. Identified as a priority institutional relationship for specialist critique of the governance documents.

## Antecedent work by the initiating steward

The conceptual foundations of the Commons Protocol's economic architecture — specifically the ATP biomimetic model applied to human value exchange, and the concept of a non-speculative, flow-oriented digital currency — were developed in a 2019 article published at intothedialectic.com and Medium, under the title *"Extinction Rebellion or Obsolescence — P2P to the Rescue?"*

That article explored participatory economics, ATP as a design model for human coin, and the potential of peer-to-peer infrastructure to make extractive economic models obsolete — drawing on Buckminster Fuller's obsolescence principle and Sahtouris's living systems framework. It predates this project by several years and represents the intellectual seed from which the Commons Protocol has grown.

The article is authored and remains so — it cannot be retroactively attributed to this project without misrepresenting its publication history. It is acknowledged here as antecedent work whose core ideas have been developed, refined, and extended in the Commons Protocol's design documents. Readers who wish to trace the intellectual lineage of the economic architecture will find it there.

## What this document does not claim

This document does not claim that any of the individuals or organisations named here endorse the Commons Protocol, are affiliated with it, or have been consulted in its development. All citations are to published, publicly available work. The intellectual debt acknowledged here is real; the relationships with the cited parties are, at this stage, aspirational rather than established.

The institutional relationships identified in the working notes — Project Drawdown, the Metagovernance Project, the Consul Project, the Fediverse and Nostr communities — represent natural points of future contact, not existing partnerships.
