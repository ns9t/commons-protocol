## Open flags — verification design

**Flag 13 — Verification sketch status**
v0.1 drafted and uploaded to design folder. Layered model combining social vouching, proof of personhood protocols, device-based zero-knowledge proofs, and progressive trust accumulation. Awaiting specialist critique.

**Flag 14 — The safety paradox**
Participants most needing privacy protection are most important to global representation. Video-based verification inaccessible to dissidents and activists in repressive regimes. Device-based zero-knowledge proofs are best current answer but require significant development. Treat as priority, not deferred problem.

**Flag 15 — Offline verification**
Large populations with intermittent or no internet connectivity have no current pathway. SMS-based systems vulnerable to state interception. Physical community verification events possible but require trusted local organisers. Open design challenge.

**Flag 16 — Key recovery**
If participant identity is a self-held keypair, loss of keypair means loss of verified identity. Recovery mechanism needed that does not recreate centralised database problem. Open design challenge.

## Open flags — evidence record design

**Flag 17 — Evidence record design status**
v0.1 drafted and uploaded to design folder. Central design question explicitly unresolved. Hybrid model proposed as working hypothesis. Awaiting specialist input from information scientists, librarians, epistemologists, and fact-checking practitioners.

**Flag 18 — The central unresolved question: Approach A vs B**
Approach A (protocol-defined credibility standards) is cleaner and more navigable but capturable. Approach B (community classification with open contestation) is more consistent with protocol principles but harder to operationalise and vulnerable to flooding. Hybrid model proposes narrow baseline exclusion standard combined with open classification above it. Decision requires specialist input — do not resolve without it.

**Flag 19 — The epistemic bias problem — named cases**
The most serious underlying challenge: in certain domains, the formally credible information environment has itself been compromised by the interests the protocol is designed to hold accountable. Named cases for future design reference:

*Andrew Wakefield / MMR vaccine:* Wakefield's original research on MMR vaccine and autism was published in the Lancet, subsequently retracted, and Wakefield's medical licence withdrawn following findings of ethical violations. This is the formally credible record. However, serious independent examination of the case — including review of the original research, the retraction process, and the campaign that preceded it — leads some credible observers to conclude that the discrediting was itself coordinated and does not accurately represent Wakefield's research, objectives, or integrity. This is not a fringe position held carelessly. The case is a concrete illustration of how the formal credentialing and retraction system can itself be captured and weaponised. Under any credibility standard deferring to formal credentials, the discrediting is the evidence and the challenge is inadmissible — the capture is complete at the level of the standard.

*Black salve / Greg Caton:* Plant-based compound with documented medicinal properties, including treatment of certain skin cancers. Regulatory and media treatment has been dismissive or actively suppressive, with practitioners imprisoned or shut down. First-hand accounts of efficacy — including from credible observers with direct experience — are not representable in the formally credible evidence record. Same structural problem as Wakefield.

*Glyphosate / Monsanto:* Extensive documented record of commercial funding of safety studies, suppression of unfavourable research, and coordinated campaigns to damage the credibility of independent researchers. Some formally peer-reviewed research in this domain served commercial rather than scientific interests. Cited in evidence record design document.

*Tobacco / pharmaceutical research:* Documented in Oreskes and Conway's *Merchants of Doubt* (2010) and Goldacre's *Bad Pharma* (2012). Formal peer review demonstrably compromised by commercial interest in specific domains.

**The structural pattern across all cases:** Capture the credentialing system, then use the credentialing system to exclude challenges. The more rigorous the credibility standard, the more completely it can be captured by actors who have already captured the credentialing systems it defers to.

**Implication for design:** Approach B's instinct — make the contestation visible rather than resolving it behind a credibility standard — may ultimately be more aligned with the protocol's values than any attempt to define credibility from above. The hybrid model's narrow baseline (excluding anonymous authorship, undisclosed material interest, and demonstrated fabrication) does not automatically exclude challenged researchers or contested science. It makes the community's disagreement about source credibility visible to participants. That visibility is imperfect but more honest than a standard that silently resolves the contest in favour of whoever captured the credentialing system.

**This is not a problem awaiting a technical solution.** It is a political problem about who controls the definition of knowledge, and it will require political as well as technical responses. Flag for future design iteration with specialist input from sociology of science, evidence-based medicine, and history of commercial influence on research.

**Flag 20 — First-mover problem in evidence records**
Whoever submits sources to an evidence record first shapes initial composition. Well-resourced actors submitting early may establish a baseline difficult to contest. Neutralising the first-mover advantage is an open design challenge.

**Flag 21 — Language and cultural equivalence in source classification**
Indigenous knowledge systems, oral traditions, and community-held evidence do not fit the standard taxonomy. How accommodated without excluding or misrepresenting their epistemological status? Open design challenge requiring input from indigenous knowledge specialists.

**Flag 22 — Retraction and correction problem**
Scientific consensus changes. Sources are retracted, corrected, superseded. Who monitors source status over time and how does the evidence record update? Open design challenge.

**Flag 23 — Scalability of community classification**
Approach B and hybrid models require participant engagement in source classification at scale not demonstrated in any existing platform. What incentive structures make this sustainable? Needs design attention before implementation.

## Open flags — architecture

**Flag 24 — Tiered access model**
Five proposed tiers: Observer, Participant, Proposer, Reviewer, Steward. Each tier has verification requirement proportionate to consequence of participation. Formally specified in verification sketch. Needs formal design document of its own.

**Flag 25 — Token / funding layer**
Architecturally and documentarily separate from signal layer. Token has utility value only — not speculative. Non-accumulative per ATP principle — tokens not spent within defined period redistributed. Closest existing model: Gitcoin with ATP constraint added. Needs its own design document. Should not appear in founding document.

**Flag 26 — Interactive globe / interconnection map**
Core interface element. Needs dedicated design document. Key questions: data sources, editorial governance, technical architecture, currency of data. Connection to Project Drawdown methodology worth exploring.

## Notes on tone and scope discipline

The project's first iteration should focus on questions with navigable evidential situations where the factual basis, while contested, is not comprehensively suppressed by the same interests the protocol is designed to hold accountable. Geopolitical abuses, environmental destruction, and resource extraction are appropriate first-iteration territory. Medical suppression, alternative science, and regulatory capture — while within the project's long-term scope and explicitly flagged as future design challenges — should not be attempted in the first iteration. Credibility before scope expansion is the correct sequencing.
